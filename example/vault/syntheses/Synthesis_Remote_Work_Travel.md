## 1. Question

Does remote work lengthen travel (longer trips) or shorten it (fewer trips)?

## 2. What the entries say

- Per-trip distance rises after the start of remote work, mostly among movers
  (p. 108-109 in [[Doe_2024_Remote_Work_Commuting_Distance_]]).
- Hybrid employees live farther from work; the gap shrinks from 6.2 to 3.0 km
  once housing prices are controlled (p. 12-14 in [[Moe_2025_Hybrid_Schedules_Residential_Relocation_]]).
- Weekly travel time falls (p. 110 in [[Doe_2024_Remote_Work_Commuting_Distance_]]).

## 3. Where they agree, where they disagree

- **Confirms:** Doe and Moe agree that distance rises, by different routes
  (panel change vs. cross-section of movers).
- **Citation trap:** the weekly travel-time result rests on self-reports that
  are inflated for long commutes and not validated for change
  (p. 9, p. 15 in [[Roe_2023_Recall_Error_Self_Reported_Commuting_]]).

## 4. Open questions

- Would diary-based travel times confirm the weekly decline?
- Does the working-paper estimate in Moe survive in print?
